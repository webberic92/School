### Permissioned vs Permissionless: Real-World Examples

#### 1. **HoneyBadgerBFT (HB‑BFT)**

HoneyBadgerBFT is designed for **permissioned systems**, where all participating nodes are *known in advance*, and trust is managed through secure channels (e.g. within a single data center, consortium, or organization). This enables:

* **Optimized communication patterns** (e.g. reliable broadcast using preconfigured PKI).
* **Assumed trust boundaries**, eliminating costly Sybil resistance and frequent identity validation.

As a result, HB‑BFT can process larger batches (like 10k txpb) efficiently, but only under controlled, permissioned conditions.
([USENIX][1])

#### 2. **Dumbo‑NG and Speeding Dumbo**

These asynchronous BFT protocols also target **permissioned environments**:

* **Dumbo‑NG** achieves extremely high throughput (e.g. tens to hundreds of thousands of tx/s) and low, stable latency under WAN deployment ([arXiv][2]).
* **Speeding Dumbo** builds on this by drastically reducing latency and doubling throughput compared to vanilla Dumbo, again under the assumption of trusted, permissioned setup with pre-established node identities and optimized batching ([NDSS Symposium][3]).

---

### Why This Doesn’t Apply to Your Setup

Your environment is **permissionless**, meaning:

* Anyone can join—no PKI or pre-approved node list.
* There is no built-in trust or high-speed, low-latency network assumptions.
* Additional consensus verifications and broadcast overhead are required for security and anti-Sybil measures.

These factors significantly **increase computational and communication costs**, especially on commodity cloud instances like c5n.xlarge.
