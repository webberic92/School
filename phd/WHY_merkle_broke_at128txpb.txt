### Merkle Failure under High Load (128 txpb)

In the 5-node `t3.medium` experiment with 128 transactions per batch, the **MERKLE protocol failed due to resource exhaustion**, while **RSA completed successfully**. This highlights a critical difference in scalability between the two protocols.

#### Why MERKLE Broke

Merkle trees grow with batch size, and at high `txpb` the system must:

* Compute `O(n log n)` SHA hashes
* Allocate `O(n)` memory for tree structures
* Manage larger buffers and queues

In contrast, **RSA performs a constant `O(1)` signature per round**, regardless of `txpb`.

At 128 txpb:

* CPU saturation (hashing)
* Memory exhaustion (tree buffers)
* Queue overflows (slower processing)

led to MERKLE's failure on low-resource nodes like `t3.medium`.

#### Broader Implication

This shows a **scalability vs decentralization tradeoff**:
As protocol load increases, only more powerful (and centralized) nodes can keep up—hurting decentralization.
